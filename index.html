Multiple Sclerosis
9.
An immune-mediated progressive disease of the CNS characterized by small patches of demyelination in the brain and spinal cord; also known as disseminated sclerosis.
10.
The age range during which onset of multiple sclerosis symptoms usually occurs.
11.
The primary imaging diagnostic tool used to visualize small plaques in multiple sclerosis and evaluate the cause and effects of treatment.

The diagnostic technique that uses CSF electrophoresis and detects abnormal IgG, able to identify MS in up to 95% of patients.

Trigeminal Neuralgia
18.
A condition of the fifth cranial nerve characterized by paroxysms of pain in the area innervated by any of its three branches; also called Tic Douloureux.
19.
The anticonvulsant drug (brand name Tegretol) that relieves pain in trigeminal neuralgia by reducing the transmission of impulses at nerve terminals.
20.
The adverse effect that must be monitored during long-term carbamazepine therapy in trigeminal neuralgia.
21.
The intracranial surgical approach used to decompress the trigeminal nerve by relieving contact between the cerebral vessel and the trigeminal nerve root entry.
22.
The percutaneous surgical procedure for trigeminal neuralgia that produces a thermal lesion on the trigeminal nerve, though it may cause dysesthesia and loss of corneal reflex.
23.
The surgical procedure for trigeminal neuralgia that disrupts large myelinated fibers of all three branches by compressing the nerve root for 1 minute using a balloon filled with contrast material.
Myasthenia Gravis
24.
A chronic autoimmune, progressive neuromuscular disease characterized by an abnormal degree of weakness and fatigability of the skeletal (voluntary) muscles.
25.
The drug (brand name Tensilon) administered IV to diagnose myasthenia gravis; facial muscle weakness and ptosis should resolve within 30 seconds of injection.
26.
The diagnostic test for myasthenia gravis in which ice is held over the patient's eye for 1 minute; a positive result is resolution of ptosis.
27.
The drug (brand name Mestinon) used as first-line therapy in myasthenia gravis to increase the relative concentration of available acetylcholine at the neuromuscular junction.
28.
The cytotoxic drug (brand name Cytoxan) used in myasthenia gravis to decrease circulating anti-acetylcholine receptor antibodies.
29.
The IV therapy administered to treat exacerbations of myasthenia gravis; abbreviated IVIG.
30.
The surgical procedure used in myasthenia gravis that can produce antigen-specific immunosuppression, though the patient may not benefit until up to 1 year after the operation.
